## Quarter 2 Project Ideas

**What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

The most interesting topic to me is using sentiment analysis to understand patient experiences from written reviews. Some reviews can contain both positive and negative feedback, which makes assigning one overall sentiment label difficult. I am interested in how different methods handle these mixed reviews and whether they can identify the specific issues patients discuss and their severity.

**Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

I would like to investigate whether analyzing sentiment separately for different aspects of care provides more useful information than assigning one label to an entire review. For example, a patient might praise their doctor but complain about scheduling or billing. I could manually label a sample of reviews by topic and sentiment, then evaluate how accurately a model identifies both. This could help distinguish satisfaction with medical care from dissatisfaction with administrative services.

**What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

I would add a more systematic error analysis to our comparison of sentiment analysis methods. Instead of only comparing overall accuracy, I would examine which types of reviews each method gets wrong, such as reviews containing mixed sentiment, medical terminology, or indirect complaints. I would also use clear labeling guidelines and have a second person independently label a subset of reviews to check whether our reference labels are consistent.

**What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

I would be interested in using topic modeling to discover recurring concerns in patient reviews, such as wait times, communication, and billing. I would also like to explore aspect-based sentiment analysis to connect positive or negative opinions to those specific topics. These techniques could make the results more informative than overall sentiment labels alone. Also, I would like to expand our analysis to include competitor data.
